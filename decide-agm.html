The last column is not one answer. It is these three assets written in the order we prefer them for that target.

When

The first column of the matrix says when a target is attacked. There are three answers:

MeaningWhat it does to the fight
ImmediatePreempts all other routine missions. The target is attacked as soon as it is positively identified, ahead of every as acquired target waiting, whatever its place on the HPTL.
As acquiredEngaged in HPTL order. When several targets are waiting, the one highest on the HPTL is engaged first, provided an asset is free and is not already committed to an immediate or a scheduled planned mission. Two targets of the same priority are engaged in the order they were acquired.
PlannedScheduled. The target is attacked at a time we choose, as part of the scheme of fires. We choose the time so that the effect is present when the rest of the plan needs it.

Two questions decide which one a target gets:

  1. Will we lose the target if it is not struck immediately? Does this target pose an immediate danger to the mission? If either is true, it is Immediate.
  2. Does this target need to be struck at a specific time? If it does, it is Planned.

Otherwise, targets may be struck as acquired, meaning they will be struck in the order of their priority.

Task 1.4 — Set the timing

NOT DONE

Apply the two questions to each HPT.

1. Engineer obstacle team
Placing charges on the bridge at this moment. If they finish, the mission is over.

Commander agrees: this preempts every other mission. If the engineer team is not engaged immediately we risk losing the bridge.

Commander disagrees: if we wait, we risk losing the bridge. Fires on the engineer team take priority over anything else in the queue.

2. ATGM section
Known to relocate every 24 hours. But the commander does not want to strike this target early, in order to achieve surprise or to prevent the enemy from replacing it.

Commander agrees: the section has been located and is unlikely to move, so we can schedule it. We will strike it in coordination with our movement, otherwise we risk the enemy sending another section that we have not located.

Commander disagrees: he worries that if we strike the ATGM section early, we risk the enemy sending another one before we clear the far side of the bridge.

3. 120 mm mortar platoon
Only findable while firing, and firing on our troops at the crossing. We must engage while we still have the tubes in sight.

Commander agrees: the mortars are an imminent danger to our soldiers at the crossing, and they can only be found while they fire, so they preempt every other mission. Waiting loses both the target and our soldiers.

Commander disagrees: the platoon moves after every mission, and it is firing on our soldiers now. Waiting in a queue means we never find the tubes again, and our soldiers stay under fire.

4. Battalion command post
Not located yet. Relocates every 24 hours. It is not stopping the crossing at this moment.

Commander agrees: the command post is not an imminent danger to our forces, and no friendly action waits on its destruction, so it does not warrant dropping a higher-priority task. We engage it in its turn on the HPTL, when an asset is free.

Commander disagrees: once we locate the command post we do not need to wait, as the enemy cannot easily regain this capability. No friendly action is contingent upon the command post being destroyed, and the commander would not risk it relocating while we wait.

Commander disagrees: the command post is mobile, and the enemy usually relocates it after 24 hours, but it is not an imminent danger to friendly forces, so it does not preempt our other missions.

What effect

The second column of the matrix says what the attack must do to the target. There are three answers. They are not three strengths of the same thing. Each one buys a different result, for a different length of time, at a different cost in ammunition.

MeaningWhat it does to the targetHow long it lasts
Suppress Limits what the target can do. The crews stay under cover and cannot fire accurately. Only while our rounds are landing. It ends the moment we stop firing.
Neutralize Disables the capability we care about and breaks the unit apart as a fighting team. Temporary. The unit can recover if we give it time.
Destroy Physical destruction of the target. Permanent. The target must be replaced before it can work again.

Task 1.5 — Choose the effect

NOT DONE

Choose the desired effect for each HPT. Use the planning notes to guide you.

Mandate only the minimum effect the mission requires. Every step up, from suppress to neutralize to destroy, costs more ammunition. The smallest effect that still serves the plan is the right one; only the commander can ask for more than the mission needs. Check each target as you finish it, and the reasoning will appear beneath it.

1. Engineer obstacle team
They are working on the bridge itself. The bridge must survive. Their primary function is to block or destroy it, and they must be stopped from completing it.

We only require that the engineers be stopped from completing their primary function, blocking or destroying the bridge, not that the team be destroyed. Neutralize takes away the capability we care about, and that is usually all the plan requires.

2. ATGM section
Dug in on high ground, looking straight down on the crossing. Commander's word: the ATGM team must not be able to affect our vehicles as they cross that chokepoint.

We only care about the ATGM section during the crossing. It only has to be unable to shoot accurately while our vehicles are on the chokepoint, and suppress buys exactly that, for exactly as long as we need it, at the lowest cost in ammunition.

3. 120 mm mortar platoon
Deep behind the river, moves after each mission. The commander requires this enemy capability removed for the duration of the mission, about a day.

The commander only requires that this enemy capability be removed for the duration of the mission, about a day. That is a temporary effect, so neutralize is enough; destroy would spend more ammunition than the mission needs.

4. Battalion command post
Assessed roughly 4 km behind the river, though not yet located. Neutralize is enough to keep the defense from coordinating while we cross, and the commander's written requirement is only to “disrupt” his command and control. The commander has decided otherwise: if we are going to strike a command post, he wants it gone for good, not one staff officer missing and one radio replaced. He accepts what that costs in ammunition.

The command post is the exception, and the mission did not make it one. Neutralize would have served: it keeps the defense from coordinating while we cross. But the commander decided that if we strike a command post he wants it gone for good, not one staff officer missing and one radio replaced. He accepts what that costs in ammunition. Only the commander can ask for more than the mission needs.

Preferred method of engagement

The last column of the matrix says what we will attack the target with. It is not one answer. It is a short list in the order we prefer it. The first choice is what we use when nothing prevents it. The second and third are there because the first will sometimes be firing another mission, out of range, or unable to fire where the target has appeared. The cell works down the list without going back to the commander.

There is no formula that produces the order for every target. It comes from what fires have to achieve against that particular target, and from what we can afford to spend doing it. The commander's fires guidance is therefore written for each high-payoff target, and it is given with the target below.

Attack guidance is not an exact science. The approved matrix may reflect the opinions of the commander. Two competent staff officers will order the same three assets differently for the same target and both will be able to defend it. The order also turns on something this page has not given you yet — where the target is, and how accurately and how recently we have located it. That is the work of the target selection standards, on the next page. Target 4 below is the clearest case: read the commander's reasoning, then ask whether you would have written the same order.

Task 1.6 — Choose the method

NOT DONE

Read the commander’s fires guidance above each target, then select the primary and alternate means of engagement. Whatever asset is left over is the tertiary means, so you do not have to record it. The engineer team is worked for you as an example. Check each target as you finish it, and the commander’s reasoning will appear beneath it.

Fires guidance. “Destruction of this target is vital to mission success. Do not risk missing. The target is likely on the road, and I would prefer less cratering for future friendly operations.”

A small team, unprotected, likely carrying explosives or mines.

Primary, OWA-UAS. I do not want to risk missing, since they are vital to friendly mission success. Likely a small team carrying explosives or mines.

Alternate, M101 105 mm howitzer battery. If I have to use indirect fire, I might as well use the cheapest and most abundant round, given the small and unprotected nature of the target.

Not located yet, and it relocates every 24 hours. Because it is unlocated we do not yet know what we are striking: it may be a large static compound of tents and vehicles, or it may be a single mobile command vehicle. Primary
Alternate

Primary, M109 155 mm battery. If the command post is a large static compound — several tents and vehicles spread over some ground — it is an area target, and the 155 covers the most ground on the first salvo. I wish to minimize the risk of missing, because if we do not meet the criteria on that first salvo the enemy displaces and we lose the opportunity.

Alternate, OWA-UAS. If instead it proves to be a single mobile command vehicle, that is a point target, and the drone buys precision and a guaranteed hit on it.

This is the order that will be argued about, and it is the one to argue about. Reverse it — drone first, then 155 — and you have a defensible matrix for a different assumption about what the command post turns out to be. What settles it is where the target is and how well we have located it, which is the next page.
